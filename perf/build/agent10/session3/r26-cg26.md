### What the pauses do at run time
- The change is output-identical: PAUSE changes no architectural state (b2r §36). Only timing changes.
- `sub_83FAF180` (TU107 L36777-36817): `ctr = 4` loop of 8 db16cyc, now 8 `simde_mm_pause()` per pass. That is 32 PAUSEs per poll, about 1.1-1.5 us on Skylake and later. Before, clang could delete the empty loop. The no-progress watchdog reads its time from guest memory (`r13+256` → `+88`, L36829/36835), not a loop count, so the pauses don't stretch it.
- Finding-10 interplay: the 8 pauses sit in the finding-10 owner function. With #24 OFF, the hook calls `__imp__sub_83FAF180` at once, so they run in both OFF and ON hook states. Each L1 poll gets slower by ≤ ~1.5 us, which delays the owner's exit by at most one poll after the GPU word moves. The lock protocol doesn't change. Less SMT pressure from the spinning thread may even help. Finding 10 is load-sensitive, so condition 5 makes this measured, not assumed.
- The other 12 sites (9 functions) are single PAUSEs or pairs. They weren't inspected individually (b2r §36); the same output-identity holds.
