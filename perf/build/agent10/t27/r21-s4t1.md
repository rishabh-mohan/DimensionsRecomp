Why it is safe under these conditions:
- **Only 4 threads change, and ABOVE_NORMAL chain threads already exist.** Value 1/3 raises the recorder (th3), the recording queue (th2) and the 2 prepare threads. The worker/coordinator is already ABOVE_NORMAL today (nw:390, N1), and it already shares g_queue_mutex and NativeRecordingMutex with NORMAL threads. #21 extends an existing practice.
- **Raised threads sleep when idle.** Every chain thread idles in a condition-variable wait (nw:161, :206, :269, nr:118, np:40).
- **No raised thread spins on a NORMAL thread.** My helper grepped all of snap1 src for spin and poll patterns (sub-r21-coderead.md §2).
  - The only spin a chain role can run is LimitFrameRate's clock spin of at most 0.5 ms (device.cpp:1817). It waits on the clock, not on a thread.
  - It is off in F1, F3 and W8b. Their arms set `native_settings_frontend=false`, `native_fps_limit=0` and `framerate_limit=0` (for example matrix-s4t1-f1.json:40-42), so LimitFrameRate returns at device.cpp:1802.
  - Every other chain wait blocks in the kernel or sleeps (the th2 drain polls every 200 µs).
- **F-CA's main case does not depend on priority.** If the handoff is in the tape that Immediate() flushes, it deadlocks at any priority. Only a tape posted earlier races the worker's re-lock (native_worker.cpp:270) against the queue thread's try_lock (:535), so #21 could change how often that case hangs, in either direction. Whether a handoff happens follows the guest's draw order and surface layouts, not scheduling.
- **Moderate change:** priority 9 vs 8 inside NORMAL_PRIORITY_CLASS. No realtime class, no boost change, no affinity.
- **Failures are visible and harmless.** SetThreadPriority and SetThreadInformation on the current-thread pseudo-handle return FALSE on error. The thread then logs `(set failed)` or `opt-out failed` (nw:358-360) and keeps running.
- **Rollback is unsetting the variable.** No rebuild is needed.
